How this works

A listing has to clear every hard requirement before it shows up anywhere on this site at all: rent between $4,000–$7,000, at least 1 bedroom, in-unit washer/dryer, a kitchen that's open or semi-open (not closed or galley), and not a coil electric stove (the old-style visible-spiral-burner kind — smooth-top electric and gas are both fine). Gowanus and Crown Heights are excluded outright regardless of anything else, and so is Upper West Side north of 96th Street — outside the comfort zone entirely, not just downweighted. None of that is weighted or negotiable — it's pass/fail.

Everything that clears those gets a match score out of 100, blending nine things:

  • Neighborhood preference — Brooklyn and Upper West Side in the 72nd–89th St sweet spot are tied at 100; UWS outside that range (but south of 96th) scores 80; everywhere else (other Manhattan, Queens/LIC) scores 30. Brooklyn and UWS tie deliberately: UWS's apparent edge turned out to be mostly proximity to a specific friend there, which the friends-commute score below already covers — holding commute fixed, UWS and Brooklyn are a genuine tie, not UWS-first. The 72nd-89th sweet spot inside UWS is a separate, real preference (not proximity-driven), so it stays as its own tier. Street cutoffs come from the actual geocoded address when it's on a numbered cross street ("West 94th Street"), or an estimate from the building's coordinates when it's on an avenue (Columbus, Amsterdam, West End, Central Park West) — StreetEasy only tags the whole stretch "Upper West Side," it doesn't say where in it a listing sits.
  • Office commute — shorter is better, scored on a sliding scale.
  • Friends commute — averaged across your four friends (Upper West Side, Morningside Heights, Hunters Point/LIC, Prospect Heights), same sliding scale.
  • Size — bigger is always better, but normalized by bedroom count (divided by the square root of bedrooms, so it's a real but weak adjustment, not a straight per-bedroom split) since the same square footage feels roomier with fewer bedrooms to share it — an 800sqft 1-bedroom is spacious, an 800sqft 2-bedroom is cramped by comparison. Below 600sqft per bedroom this goes negative instead of just bottoming out at 0, so a genuinely too-small unit actively drags the overall score down rather than merely failing to help it — otherwise a great location could paper over a unit that's just too small. Listings without sqft in the source data (most of them) get the median real sqft for that bedroom count instead of a guess or a flat default, so a missing value doesn't quietly become a penalty or a windfall.
  • Living room — binary for now (small or not, from the same photo-based check that already flags it elsewhere): a small living room scores 0, anything else scores 100. Came up repeatedly across directed listing reviews as the single biggest drag on an otherwise-good listing — entertaining space matters enough to weight it comparably to size, not just as a tiebreaker.
  • Kitchen size — also from the photo-based check, judging the fixed counter/cabinet run and appliances rather than open-vs-closed layout (that's a separate hard requirement above) — explicitly not counting a kitchen island or peninsula toward size, since that's dining/seating furniture, not kitchen work space: large scores 100, small scores 0, standard or unclassifiable scores 50. The single highest-weighted dimension (16%) per direct instruction — "kitchen size is very important."
  • Condo — a boost, not a hard requirement: condos score 100, everything else (rental buildings, co-ops, mixed-use, multi-family) scores a neutral 50, so non-condos aren't penalized, condos just get lifted above the baseline. Detected directly from StreetEasy's own listing text. Added per explicit instruction rather than an observed review pattern, weighted lightly (6%) since it's a boost rather than a core priority.
  • Value — price per real-or-estimated square foot (reusing the same bedroom-typical sqft estimate as the size dimension above when real sqft isn't listed, so it degrades to a price-per-bedroom-equivalent rather than a separate guess). Cheap end ($5.50/sqft) scores 100, expensive end ($9.50/sqft) scores 0, calibrated against the real range across qualifying listings with known sqft. Added after noticing the highest-ranked listing at the time ($6,900 for a 788sqft 1-bedroom, $8.76/sqft) had nothing pulling its score down for being expensive within budget.
  • Ground floor — a small penalty: anything else scores 100, a suspected ground-floor unit scores 0. Detected from StreetEasy's own unit-number convention (unit numbers conventionally start with the floor number, so "#1B" reads as floor 1, while "#11B"/"#100" etc. don't match since a digit right after the leading "1" means floor 10+) — a heuristic on a naming convention, not a certainty; buildings that don't number units by floor simply won't get flagged. Added after "ground floor could be a problem" recurred across 3 independent listings, weighted lightest of the nine (4%) per direct instruction for "a small penalty."

The default weighting is 15% neighborhood, 15% office, 12% friends, 10% size, 12% living room, 16% kitchen size, 6% condo, 10% value, 4% ground floor. If that balance feels off, adjust it below; it re-sorts the New and All Qualifying tabs immediately and remembers your setting on this device.
